#### Mode: `auto` (chosen: `rag`, 5563.68 ms, 7206 tok, $0.019440)

The initial starting dose of metformin for adults with Type 2 diabetes is 500 mg orally twice a day or 850 mg once a day, given with meals. The dose can be increased in increments of 500 mg weekly or 850 mg every two weeks based on glycemic control and tolerability, up to a maximum dose of 2550 mg per day, administered in divided doses. For doses above 2000 mg, it may be better tolerated if given three times a day with meals. For the extended-release formulation, GLUCOPHAGE XR, the recommended starting dose is 500 mg orally once daily with the evening meal, with dose increments of 500 mg weekly up to a maximum of 2000 mg once daily. If glycemic control is not achieved with 2000 mg once daily, a trial of 1000 mg twice daily may be considered, or switching to regular GLUCOPHAGE at total daily doses up to 2550 mg in divided doses may be necessary.

### What is type 2 diabetes?

Type 2 diabetes is a condition in which your body does not make enough insulin, and
the insulin that your body produces does not work as well as it should. Your body can
also make too much sugar. When this happens, sugar (glucose) builds up in the
blood. This can lead to serious medical problems.

The main goal of treating diabetes is to lower your blood sugar to a normal level.

High blood sugar can be lowered by diet and exercise, and by certain medicines when
necessary.

Talk to your healthcare provider about how to prevent, recognize, and take care of
low blood sugar (hypoglycemia), high blood sugar (hyperglycemia), and problems

Metformin is often the first-line treatment for type 2 diabetes due to its efficacy in reducing diabetes-related endpoints and overall mortality, particularly in overweight patients. Sulfonylureas are associated with a higher risk of hypoglycemia compared to other oral antidiabetic drugs. SGLT2 inhibitors have shown beneficial effects on cardiovascular outcomes, including reduced rates of cardiovascular death and hospitalization for heart failure. Overall, while sulfonylureas and insulin are effective in lowering blood glucose, their higher risk of hypoglycemia makes DPP-4 inhibitors and SGLT2 inhibitors more favorable for elderly patients, particularly those with cardiovascular concerns or renal impairment.
