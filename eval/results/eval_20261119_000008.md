#### Mode: `auto` (chosen: `hybrid`, 9951.72 ms, 6359 tok, $0.019458)

Long-term metformin therapy can complicate the clinical assessment of diabetic polyneuropathy primarily through the risk of vitamin B12 deficiency. Metformin has been shown to decrease serum vitamin B12 levels in approximately 7% of patients, potentially due to interference with B12 absorption from the B12-intrinsic factor complex. This deficiency can lead to anemia and neurological issues, which may mimic or exacerbate symptoms of diabetic polyneuropathy, complicating its assessment.

To monitor and manage this risk, it is recommended to measure hematologic parameters annually and vitamin B12 levels every 2 to 3 years in patients on metformin therapy. For neuropathy screening, if neuropathy has not been previously detected, it is recommended to screen every one to two years based on individual risk assessment.

In patients with type 2 diabetes and heart failure taking metformin, the co-administration of certain drugs can affect metformin exposure and the risk of lactic acidosis. Furosemide, when co-administered with metformin, increases the area under the curve (AUC) and maximum concentration (Cmax) of metformin by 9% and 22%, respectively, but no dosing adjustments are required. Cimetidine significantly increases metformin's AUC and Cmax by 40% and 61%, respectively, which may increase the risk of lactic acidosis due to higher systemic exposure. Topiramate, a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor, increases metformin's AUC by 25% and Cmax by 17%, and may cause metabolic acidosis, further increasing the risk of lactic acidosis.

Heart failure itself is a risk factor for metformin-associated lactic acidosis, especially in acute congestive heart failure with hypoperfusion and hypoxemia. Therefore, careful monitoring is advised when metformin is used in patients with heart failure.

Regarding the prioritization of SGLT2 inhibitors in patients with heart failure, these agents, such as dapagliflozin, have been shown to reduce the risk of hospitalization due to heart failure. This makes SGLT2 inhibitors a favorable option in managing type 2 diabetes patients with concurrent heart failure.
